The Top 3 Browsers for Privacy

Mullvad Browser

Mullvad Browser was made by the Mullvad VPN team and the Tor Project. Its main goal is to stop websites from fingerprinting you. Fingerprinting is when a site uses small details about your computer (screen size, fonts, browser version) to figure out who you are, even if you're not logged in. Mullvad makes everyone look the same so this doesn't work.

It comes with privacy settings already turned on, so you don't have to do anything. Use this one if you want strong privacy without messing with settings.

Brave

Brave runs on the same engine as Chrome, so every site works the same way you're used to. The difference is that Brave blocks ads and trackers automatically. It also has a built-in private mode that uses Tor.

If you're coming from Chrome, this is the easiest switch. It looks almost identical and your Chrome extensions still work.

Firefox

Firefox is the only big browser not made on Chrome's engine. That matters because it stops Google from controlling the whole web. Out of the box, Firefox is okay for privacy but not amazing. You have to turn on a few things first:

  • Set Enhanced Tracking Protection to Strict
  • Turn on HTTPS-Only mode
  • Turn off telemetry in the settings
  • Install uBlock Origin

Once you do those, Firefox is one of the best browsers you can use.

What to Avoid

Chrome is made by Google. Google makes money from ads, and they use your browsing data to target those ads. Everything you do in Chrome can be linked to your Google account.

Edge is made by Microsoft. It sends data back to Microsoft and pushes their AI and Bing search constantly.

Safari is better than Chrome and Edge but it's still tied to your Apple ID and only works on Apple devices.

The one thing to take from this page: stop using Chrome.
